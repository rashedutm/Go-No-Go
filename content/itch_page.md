## Description (paste into the description box)

**"Go/No-Go" is the call every flight controller makes before a real launch. In this game, you earn it.**

You lead a robotic space mission from start to finish:

🛠️ **Build** your spacecraft in a 3D hangar: choose the rocket, frame, solar panels or nuclear power, battery, instruments and antenna. Watch the budget, mass, power and data bars react to every choice.

✅ **Pass the Go/No-Go poll.** Four flight controllers check your design. Every NO-GO tells you what to fix.

🚀 **Launch**, then steer through asteroids (or space debris near Earth). Every hit damages your spacecraft.

🖥️ **Fly it from Mission Control**, a first-person 3D control room. Solar storms, dying batteries, full memory and surprise discoveries pop up. Walk to the blinking console and act in time.

📊 **Debrief:** get scored on science, survival and budget, and find out which **real NASA mission** your design is most like.

**5 missions:** Fire Watch (Earth orbit) → Moon Mapper → Red Planet Weather (Mars) → Giant's Secret (Jupiter) → Ringworld (Saturn). The farther you go, the weaker the sunlight, the longer the radio delay and the harsher the radiation.

**There is no perfect spacecraft.** Every choice helps one thing and hurts another, just like real mission design.

### Controls
- **Mouse** for menus, the hangar and looking around
- **WASD / arrow keys** to steer the launch and walk in Mission Control
- **E** to use a console · **1–9** for console actions · **Space** to pause · **1/2/3** for time speed
- **Esc** for the menu · **F11** for fullscreen · **M** to mute

Best on a desktop or laptop (Chrome, Edge or Firefox). Click once inside the game to start the sound.

### About
Made by **Team Kakapo** (Rashed, Emon, Danial) for the **NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026**, challenge *"Space Mission Design Game"*.

Built on NASA resources: CubeSat 101, "Design and Develop Science Missions" (NASA SmallSat Institute), the NASA Mission Design Lab, and real mission facts from science.nasa.gov. Numbers are simplified for play but kept in realistic proportion.

Made with Godot 4.7. AI tools (Claude by Anthropic) helped write code and text. Not an official NASA product.
