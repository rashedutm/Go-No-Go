# Go/No-Go: 3-minute pitch video script

Slides: the "Go/No-Go Pitch Video Slides" deck (ask Member A for the link). The same words are in each slide's speaker notes.
Total: about 3:00. Speak calmly. About 430 words in all.

| Time | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Slide 1: Cover** | "Hi, we're team [team name]. For the Space Mission Design Game challenge, we built Go/No-Go: a game that runs in your web browser, where you design a robotic space mission, launch it, and fly it yourself, from Earth orbit all the way to Saturn." |
| 0:15–0:35 | **Slide 2: The problem** | "Real NASA missions are a balancing act. Every instrument you add means more science, but also more mass, more power, more data, and more money. Engineers learn these trade-offs over years. We wanted anyone to feel them in a few minutes, by making the decisions themselves." |
| 0:35–0:45 | **Slide 3: How it plays** | "Here's how one mission works, in six steps." |
| 0:45–0:50 | Gameplay: campaign map | "We pick Mars: Red Planet Weather." |
| 0:50–1:15 | Gameplay: hangar | "In the 3D hangar, four stations, Director, Engineer, Science and Comms, choose the rocket, frame, solar panels, battery, instruments and antenna. The bars at the top show budget, mass, power and data. Every choice helps one thing and hurts another." |
| 1:15–1:27 | Gameplay: Go/No-Go poll | "Like a real launch, each flight controller calls GO or NO-GO. A NO-GO tells you exactly what to fix." |
| 1:27–1:42 | Gameplay: launch + asteroid run | "Then we launch, and steer through an asteroid field on the way." |
| 1:42–2:07 | Gameplay: Mission Control | "Now we fly the mission from a first-person Mission Control room. Problems pop up, like solar storms, failing batteries or surprise discoveries. Walk to the right console and decide. On Easy you get choices. On Normal and Hard you fix it with the right tool." |
| 2:07–2:15 | Gameplay: debrief | "At the end, we're scored on science, survival and budget, and matched to the real NASA mission ours is most like. Here it's the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter." |

## Recording tips

1. **Record the gameplay first** (about 2 minutes raw, from the itch.io version, sound on). Windows: **Win + Alt + R** starts and stops Xbox Game Bar recording. OBS works too.
   - Easy difficulty, Mars mission. In the hangar, press **Load example** so the build is fast.
   - In Mission Control, press **3** for top speed and wait for an alert, then fix it at the blinking console.
2. **Record the voice separately** (phone voice recorder is fine, in a quiet room), reading the table above.
3. **Edit in CapCut or Clipchamp:** slides (export the deck as PDF or images) → gameplay → slides. Put the voice on top, and keep the game sound low (about 20%) under the voice.
4. Fill in **[team name]** on slide 1 and the **itch.io link** on slide 6 before exporting.
5. Export as 1080p MP4, and check that it is **3:00 or less**.
